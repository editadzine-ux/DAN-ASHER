Meet Dr. Dan Asher

Meet the mind
behind the method.

Dan combines research into tacit knowledge, years of organizational leadership and hands-on work with experts across professions.

Before leading his consulting practice, Dan served as a knowledge management and learning innovation leader in a government organization. His experience spans management, organizational learning and the interpersonal skills needed to understand people’s work and help them share what they know.

His doctoral research at the University of Haifa focused on tacit knowledge in professions that rely on interpersonal interaction. He explored how expert knowledge can be elicited and structured to address organizational knowledge loss. His two research articles examine the layers of tacit knowledge and ways to elicit expertise from interpersonal interactions.

Dan has worked with more than 100 experts on knowledge extraction projects. He describes himself as a “pracademic”: someone who connects theory with organizational practice. His work combines curiosity about people and technology with a practical interest in how knowledge supports real decisions.

Teaching your people to do the work

Dan co-created and led sessions in the Knowledge Management Global Network’s 2024 online course, “Leveraging the Value Creation of Tacit Knowledge.” His courses connect methods with participants’ actual organizational challenges. In the extraction course, employees work with an expert and develop a knowledge retention project as they learn. His advisory work complements this training with organizational assessments and KM technology requirements.

At Israel Aerospace Industries, an expert knowledge project developed into a broader engineering knowledge initiative, connecting a structured knowledge core with a generative AI assistant.

A few common questions

Before
we start.

Is this relevant only when experts retire?

Retirement is one reason to capture expert knowledge. The same work can support role changes, succession, onboarding, everyday learning and the development of knowledge resources for AI. Capturing expertise while it is still in use gives others time to learn from it and test it in practice.

What if experts find it difficult to explain what they know?

That is one of the main reasons to use a structured extraction process. We explore real cases, decisions, observations and examples to uncover knowledge that may not emerge in a general interview or in response to a request to write a manual.

Can you work with our existing KM platforms?

Yes. We assess the current environment and define how knowledge should be organized, found, shared and maintained. Where appropriate, this includes requirements for SharePoint and Microsoft 365 and coordination with your IT team or technology provider.

How does expert knowledge contribute to generative AI?

Expert knowledge can add reasoning, exceptions and context that are absent from existing documents. We help structure and validate those knowledge assets, document their sources and connect them with the intended AI use case. We also define how to validate and maintain the knowledge and review the resulting AI outputs.

Can our own team learn to do this?

Yes. Practical courses and workshops help your employees develop the skills to elicit, structure and transfer knowledge. Programs can be built around a real expert knowledge project, so participants create useful resources for their organization as they learn.
